Mashadi Jadid Households of 1878

خانوارهای جدید مشهد، ۱۲۹۵ قمری

The Mashad census of 1295 AH marked 114 households with the word جدید (Jadid, “new”), the label for the Jews of the city who had been forced to convert. Each row below is one household, with the Farsi as printed and an English partner beside it. Search by name, title, or trade.

    About the source

    Zeyn al-Abedin Mirza Qajar, Nofus-e Arz-e Aqdas ya Mardom-e Mashhad-e Qadim, edited by Mehdi Seyyedi (Mashhad: Sazman-e Farhangi-ye Shahrdari-ye Mashhad, 1382 SH / 2003). The tables are the editor’s typeset edition of the 1295 AH register.

    The register gives given names and titles only. It does not say which family a household belongs to.

    How to read it

    • Each Farsi column has an English partner. Use the language buttons to hide either.
    • Summary shows name, title, trade, class, and household size. All counts adds men, women, elderly, young adults, boys, girls, and infants.
    • “Wealthy” and “commoner” (رعایا, literally “subjects”) are the register’s own class marks. Commoner means a household not in state or shrine service. The register also has a column for poor households. It is not reproduced here.
    • The editor’s introduction gives different totals for some categories: 54 wealthy households (the rows give 80), and 44 Mollas, 10 Karbala’is, 6 Aghas and 4 Hajis (the rows give 56, 9, 5 and 6). This table reports what the rows say.
    • Row 935’s trade word is unresolved. A Persian reader could not tell whether it is a trade or a family name, and the editor’s own table marks it with a question mark.
    • Rows 456 and 1261 have a printed total that does not match their parts. We kept the printed figure and flagged it. Click “note” on a row to read why.
    • The English names are transliterations, not source text.
